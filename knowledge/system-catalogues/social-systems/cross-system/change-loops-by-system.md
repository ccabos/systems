# Change Loops by System

Applies `knowledge/se-techniques/change-loops/` across the catalogue.
Where `remedies-case-studies.md` asks *what fixes the loop*, this file
asks the change-loop question about the same thirteen remedies: **who
held the actuator that installed them.**

The per-system inventory for the corporation, with latencies and
resistance, is in
`../corporation/change-loop-analysis.md`. This file records only the
actuator, because that is the column that turns out to carry a
cross-system result.

## The actuator of each catalogued remedy

"Outside" and "inside" are relative to the *control structure being
repaired*, not to the country or the culture. A German parliament is
outside the German military's chain of command; a Church council is
inside the Church's own hierarchy.

## The result

**Twelve of the thirteen were installed by an actuator outside the
control structure they repair. The thirteenth is the one this
catalogue records as partially reversed.**

That is not a coincidence and it is not about the Church. It is what
the change-loop technique predicts. A captured loop is one in which
the controller is also the interested party
(`../../../se-techniques/control-structures/dangerous-patterns.md`,
Pattern 1). The rule that would repair it is held by that same
controller. So an internal actuator is, by construction, an actuator
that can also revoke — and Vatican II is the catalogue's instance of
exactly that happening.

This derives a rule the project previously stated as a precondition.
The third remedy precondition requires that a fix survive a change of
leadership. The actuator column says *why* it so often does not: where
the installing actuator sits inside the repaired loop, survival
depends on the goodwill of the actor the remedy constrains.

## What follows for reform design

An institution cannot repair a captured loop from inside it. Where no
outside position exists, one must be either **supplied** — a
legislature, a court, a regulator, a funder, an exchange — or
**manufactured**, which is what a constituent assembly is: a body
placed deliberately outside the ordinary legislative loop precisely so
that it can rewrite the rules that loop runs on. The Basic Law cases
are the clearest instance in the catalogue, and they required the
prior collapse of the system being repaired.

Two practical readings follow:

- Diagnosing a captured loop and proposing the right remedy is not
  the end of an analysis. Naming the actuator is, and if the actuator
  sits inside the loop, the analysis has not finished.
- Where the remedy must come from outside, the question becomes what
  supplies the outside actor with the capacity to act. The corporation
  analysis separates two sources — a precipitating scandal and a
  standing organised interest — and notes that neither supplies the
  analysis, only the capacity.

## Limits

- **The column is coarse.** It records who signed, not the campaign,
  advocacy or litigation that preceded the signature. The
  Wehrbeauftragter and the US military-justice reform both followed
  sustained pressure that the table does not show.
- **Selection.** The catalogue records remedies that were installed
  and afterwards studied. Internal reform attempts that failed leave
  no comparable record, so "twelve of thirteen" is a statement about
  the documented set, not a base rate.
- **Jurisdictional narrowness.** The catalogued remedies are
  overwhelmingly German, Anglo-American and Roman Catholic. Systems
  with different constitutional traditions may distribute actuators
  differently.
- **One inventory only.** Only the corporation has a full lever
  inventory with latencies. The actuator column generalises; the
  latency and resistance findings so far do not.
