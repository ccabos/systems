# Wikipedia / Open-Source Project — Sources

Citations for the claims made in this folder. Keys not expanded here
resolve in `knowledge/references/bibliography.md`.

## Primary sources

- Wikipedia policy pages: *Five Pillars*, *Verifiability*, *No
  Original Research*, *Arbitration Policy* — R3 and R5/R8 in the
  system's own constitutional text.
- Python PEP 13 (*Python Language Governance*, 2018) — a written
  record of a backstop authority being redesigned live after BDFL
  resignation; the clearest primary document for the R8 claim.
- Linux kernel *Documentation/process/* tree and the Linux
  Foundation Technical Advisory Board charter — trust hierarchy (R4)
  and dispute escalation in the software variant.

## Secondary sources

- [Raymond1999] Raymond, E.S. (1999). *The Cathedral and the
  Bazaar.* O'Reilly. The founding self-description of the open
  review model (R1–R3); read as advocacy, corrected by the empirical
  work below.
- [Benkler2006] Benkler, Y. (2006). *The Wealth of Networks.* Yale
  University Press. Commons-based peer production as a third
  production mode — the theoretical account of why VP1 (authority by
  demonstrated contribution) can substitute for both hierarchy and
  market.
- [Freeman1972] Freeman, J. (1972). "The Tyranny of
  Structurelessness." *The Second Wave*, 2(1). The named source of
  the structurelessness failure mode in this folder's observations:
  absent formal structure, informal unaccountable elites form.
- [Forte2009] Forte, A., Larco, V. & Bruckman, A. (2009).
  "Decentralization in Wikipedia Governance." *Journal of Management
  Information Systems*, 26(1), 49–72. Field study of how Wikipedia
  actually installed and distributed its governance layer — the
  empirical basis for the backstop-authority observation.
- [ShawHill2014] Shaw, A. & Hill, B.M. (2014). "Laboratories of
  Oligarchy? How the Iron Law Extends to Peer Production." *Journal
  of Communication*, 64(2), 215–238. Quantitative test of
  [Michels1911] on 683 wikis: oligarchic drift appears *even under*
  VP1 — the strongest evidence that the backstop observation is a
  law-like tendency, not an implementation accident.
