# School — Sources

Citations for the claims made in this folder. Keys not expanded here
resolve in `knowledge/references/bibliography.md`.

## Primary sources

- Compulsory-education statutes (e.g. the Schulpflicht provisions of
  the German Länder school acts; the UK Education Act 1996) — the
  legal basis of P9 (compulsory attendance).
- National curricula and examination regulations — R2/R3 in force.

## Secondary sources

- [MeyerRowan1977] — the decoupling paper's original empirical
  material is *schools*: formal structures maintained for legitimacy
  (inspection-ready paperwork, credentialed staff) operating loosely
  coupled from classroom practice. Directly relevant to any
  claimed-vs-operating reading of this system.
- [TyackCuban1995] Tyack, D. & Cuban, L. (1995). *Tinkering toward
  Utopia: A Century of Public School Reform.* Harvard University
  Press. A century of US reform cycles: goal-level reforms
  repeatedly fail while the "grammar of schooling" persists —
  independent historical support for the reuse gradient /
  start-at-the-bottom principle in `../cross-system/principles.md`.
- [Sahlberg2011] Sahlberg, P. (2011). *Finnish Lessons: What Can the
  World Learn from Educational Change in Finland?* Teachers College
  Press. The standard account of the Finnish reform cited in the
  structural observations. Note it is a participant's account;
  causal attribution of Finland's PISA results to teacher
  qualification specifically remains contested in the comparative
  literature.
- [Campbell1979] — high-stakes testing is Campbell's own running
  example of indicator corruption; the G1/G3 crowding-out
  observation is a restatement of his law.

## Data sources

- OECD PISA — the cross-jurisdiction outcome data behind any
  comparative claim about school systems, including Finland's rise
  and partial decline across cycles.
