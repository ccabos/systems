# Change Loops — Sources

Full entries and citation keys resolve in
`knowledge/references/bibliography.md`.

## The implementation tradition

The gap between a decision and its effect has its own literature,
developed in policy analysis rather than in systems or safety
engineering. This technique is largely a control-structure framing of
its results.

- **[PressmanWildavsky1973]** — the founding study. Its subject is a
  federal economic development programme in Oakland that was funded,
  agreed and publicised, and then produced almost nothing. The
  analytical move that matters here is counting the *clearance
  points*: each actor whose agreement the programme required, and the
  cumulative probability of passing all of them. That is an actuator
  inventory in all but name.
- **[Lipsky1980]** — why the last step dominates. Caseworkers,
  inspectors and teachers exercise discretion under caseload pressure,
  and the aggregate of that discretion, not the statute, is what the
  public meets. Explains why effect latency is not simply
  administrative delay: the rule has to reach a person who will
  interpret it.
- **[MazmanianSabatier1983]** — the systematising treatment: the
  conditions under which an implementation chain holds, including
  tractability of the problem, the statute's own structuring capacity,
  and the stability of political support over time. The third of those
  is the horizon mismatch under another name.
- **[WilsonJQ1989]** — why agencies behave as they do, from the
  inside: the distribution of discretion, what the organisation
  rewards, and why formal authority over an agency is not the same as
  control of it.

## Already used elsewhere in the project

- **[Meadows1999]** — ranks the power to change system structure as
  the fourth-strongest leverage point, above the rules themselves.
  The change loop is that leverage point treated as an object of
  analysis.
- **[Ashby1956]** — requisite variety, applied here to the reformer
  rather than to the operating controller.
- **[Andrews2017]**, **[MeyerRowan1977]** — isomorphic mimicry and
  institutional forms decoupled from function: the behavioural
  signature the horizon mismatch predicts.
- **[Scott1998]**, **[Lindblom1959]** — why goal-first redesign fails
  and incremental change succeeds; both are cited for the reform
  corollary in
  `knowledge/system-catalogues/social-systems/cross-system/principles.md`
  and belong to the same argument.

## A gap worth stating

This project came to institutions from safety engineering, not from
public administration. Until this technique was added, none of the
implementation literature above was cited anywhere in the
bibliography's ninety-nine entries. Where the two traditions describe
the same phenomenon, the implementation sources are older and the
evidence base is larger; this folder should be read as the junction
between them rather than as an independent result.
