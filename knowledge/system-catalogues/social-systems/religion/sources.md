# Religion — Sources

Citations for the claims made in this folder (`se-decomposition.md`,
`overview.md`, `applied-se-analysis.md`). Keys not expanded here
resolve in `knowledge/references/bibliography.md`.

## Primary sources

- Sacred texts (P2) and canon law / halakha / fiqh compilations (P6)
  — the system's own constitutional documents.
- Second Vatican Council documents, especially *Lumen Gentium*
  (1964) — the structural-opening reform analysed as a partial
  rung-elevation in `../cross-system/remedies-case-studies.md` §3.
- John Jay College of Criminal Justice (2004, 2011). *The Nature and
  Scope of Sexual Abuse of Minors by Catholic Priests and Deacons in
  the United States 1950–2002* and *The Causes and Context* study.
  USCCB. The primary quantitative record behind the UCA-R1
  (concealment) analysis.
- [RoyalCommission2017] — the Australian evidentiary record for the
  same failure mode, across multiple denominations.

## Secondary sources

- [Durkheim1912] Durkheim, É. (1912). *Les formes élémentaires de la
  vie religieuse.* The functional account of G3 (social cohesion and
  collective identity through shared ritual) — the sociological
  original of the L1-serves-G3 claim.
- [Berger1967] Berger, P.L. (1967). *The Sacred Canopy: Elements of
  a Sociological Theory of Religion.* Doubleday. The
  meaning-provision function (G1, R1, R2) as a socially constructed
  and maintained "plausibility structure" — including why the
  structure must defend itself against disconfirming input, which is
  the sociological reading of the self-sealing pattern.
- [Weber1922] — routinisation of charisma: how R4 (authority
  structure) emerges from founder-charisma and hardens into office;
  the standard account of the transition this folder's control
  structure presupposes.
- [OMalley2008] O'Malley, J.W. (2008). *What Happened at Vatican
  II.* Harvard University Press. The historical assessment behind
  the "partially reversed in subsequent decades" caveat on the
  Vatican II remedy.

## Data sources

- John Jay (2004) incidence data; Royal Commission case-study
