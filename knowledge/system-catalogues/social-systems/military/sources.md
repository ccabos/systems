# Military — Sources

Citations for the claims made in this folder. Keys not expanded here
resolve in `knowledge/references/bibliography.md`.

## Primary sources

- Clausewitz, C. von (1832). *Vom Kriege.* The subordination of
  military means to political ends — the classical statement of the
  MR3 requirement, written from inside the profession.
- Gesetz über den Wehrbeauftragten des Deutschen Bundestages (1957)
  and the Innere Führung doctrine (Zentrale Dienstvorschrift
  A-2600/1) — the statutory implementation of the independent
  rung-3 channel discussed in the rung section.
- U.S. National Defense Authorization Act (2022), §§531–539E — the
  transfer of serious-felony prosecution to independent Special
  Trial Counsel (UCA-M2 remedy).

## Secondary sources

- [Huntington1957] Huntington, S.P. (1957). *The Soldier and the
  State.* Harvard University Press. "Objective civilian control" —
  the canonical theory of how MR1 (internal hierarchy) and MR3
  (civilian subordination) can coexist; the claimed-rung structure
  of the modern professional military is essentially Huntington's.
- [Janowitz1960] Janowitz, M. (1960). *The Professional Soldier.*
  Free Press. The competing sociological account: convergence with
  civilian society rather than separation; predicts different
  failure modes at the civil–military interface.
- [Feaver2003] Feaver, P.D. (2003). *Armed Servants: Agency,
  Oversight, and Civil-Military Relations.* Harvard University
  Press. Principal–agent formalisation of the civil–military loop —
  monitoring ("police patrols vs fire alarms") is precisely the
  feedback-channel design problem the rung section states in
  control-structure terms.

## Data sources

- Annual reports of the Wehrbeauftragter (Bundestag printed papers)
  — the actual rung-3 output of the independent channel, publicly
  archived since 1959.
