# Democracy (Modern Constitutional Republic) — Sources

Citations for the claims made in this folder. Keys not expanded here
resolve in `knowledge/references/bibliography.md`.

## Primary sources

- Grundgesetz für die Bundesrepublik Deutschland (1949) — the
  post-Weimar engineered constitution analysed in
  `../cross-system/remedies-case-studies.md` §1: constructive vote
  of no confidence (Art. 67), party ban (Art. 21), constitutional
  court (Art. 93–94).
- [Madison1788] *The Federalist Papers* — the design rationale for
  RR3 (separation of powers) written by the designers themselves.

## Secondary sources

- [Dahl1971] Dahl, R.A. (1971). *Polyarchy: Participation and
  Opposition.* Yale University Press. The standard operationalisation
  of RR1 + RR6: which observable institutions must be present before
  a system counts as democratic. The closest thing the political
  science literature has to a requirements specification for this
  system.
- [Lijphart1999] Lijphart, A. (1999). *Patterns of Democracy.* Yale
  University Press. Empirical comparison of 36 democracies —
  documents the *variation points* within the type (majoritarian vs
  consensus bindings of the same functional slots).
- [LevitskyZiblatt2018] Levitsky, S. & Ziblatt, D. (2018). *How
  Democracies Die.* Crown. Contemporary failure modes; matches the
  UCA-D3/UCA-D4 analysis (legal capture of referees and information
  environment) with recent comparative cases.
- [AcemogluRobinson2012] on why copying RP-level institutions
  without the underlying power distribution fails.

## Data sources

- V-Dem (Varieties of Democracy) Institute, University of
  Gothenburg — https://www.v-dem.net — panel data on the RR1–RR6
  requirements per country per year; the natural rung-3 check on any
  claim this folder makes about a specific democracy.
