# Kingdom — Sources

Citations for the claims made in this folder. Keys not expanded here
resolve in `knowledge/references/bibliography.md`.

**Scope note.** The decomposition is a Weberian *ideal type* — a
composite of pre-constitutional European and comparable monarchies —
not a description of any single historical kingdom. Claims should be
read as claims about the type; any specific kingdom will deviate.

## Primary sources

- House laws and succession acts (e.g. the Salic law tradition,
  the English Act of Settlement 1701) — the KR1 succession
  requirement in its original legal form.

## Secondary sources

- [Weber1922] Weber, M. (1922). *Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft.* The
  ideal type itself: *traditional authority*, patrimonial
  administration (KL1, KL5), and the legitimation function of
  dynastic tradition (KG4, KR6).
- [Kantorowicz1957] Kantorowicz, E.H. (1957). *The King's Two
  Bodies: A Study in Mediaeval Political Theology.* Princeton
  University Press. How dynastic continuity (KG1) was theorised so
  that the crown survives the mortal king — the medieval original of
  "institutional continuity beyond any individual".
- [Bloch1939] Bloch, M. (1939/40). *La société féodale.* (English:
  *Feudal Society*, 1961.) The loyalty apparatus (KR2) and
  territorial administration through personal bonds (KL5) as they
  actually operated.

## Data sources

None. The rung assessment in `se-decomposition.md` is an analytic
judgment against the ideal type, not a measurement.
