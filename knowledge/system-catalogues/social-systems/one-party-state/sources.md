# One-Party State — Sources

Citations for the claims made in this folder. Keys not expanded here
resolve in `knowledge/references/bibliography.md`.

## Primary sources

- Constitution of the Communist Party of China; Constitution of the
  PRC (1982, am. 2018), Art. 1 — the OR1 "leading role" requirement
  in force today.
- Constitution of the USSR (1977), Art. 6 — the same requirement in
  its Soviet form.

## Secondary sources

- [Arendt1951] Arendt, H. (1951). *The Origins of Totalitarianism.*
  Schocken. The classical analysis of OR2 (party-state parallel
  hierarchies) and OR3 (ideological conformity) as a distinct
  system type rather than a corrupt democracy.
- [Kornai1992] Kornai, J. (1992). *The Socialist System: The
  Political Economy of Communism.* Princeton University Press. The
  definitive account of OL5 (planning subsystem) and of why its
  feedback channels systematically fail — the shortage economy as
  an information problem, from an economist who lived inside it.
- [Svolik2012] Svolik, M.W. (2012). *The Politics of Authoritarian
  Rule.* Cambridge University Press. Formal and empirical treatment
  of the regime's two control problems (elite power-sharing and mass
  control) — the modern political-science formalisation of the
  monitoring pathologies this folder describes structurally.
- [Dikotter2010], [Yang2012] — archival evidence for the Great Leap
  Forward feedback collapse discussed in
  `../cross-system/justification-rungs-by-system.md`.

## Data sources

- V-Dem indices (see `../democracy/sources.md`) cover one-party
  states with the same instruments, enabling claimed-vs-operating
  comparisons on measured variables.
