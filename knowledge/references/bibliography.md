# Bibliography

Shared bibliography referenced by any knowledge or project file. Use
`[AuthorYear]` keys for inline citations. Entries are grouped by
theme; within a theme they are ordered by year. Per-technique
`references.md` files (e.g. under `se-techniques/stamp/`,
`se-techniques/justification-rungs/`, `se-techniques/order-theory-and-fca/`)
give annotated guidance on the sources most relevant to that
technique; this file is the flat, citable superset.

A note on intellectual honesty: little in this project is original.
The working definition of a system restates a consensus running from
[Bertalanffy1968] through [Meadows2008]; the control-structure
diagnostics restate results from cybernetics [Ashby1956; Beer1972],
organisational learning [ArgyrisSchon1978], and safety science
[Rasmussen1997; Leveson2012]; the justificatory-rung ladder is a
synthesis of well-known positions in epistemology and political
theory (see `../se-techniques/justification-rungs/references.md`).
The project's contribution is arrangement and application, not
discovery — and where a claim here goes beyond the cited literature,
the citation trail below is what makes that visible.

## Format

```
[AuthorYear]
  Author, A. (Year). *Title*. Publisher. DOI or URL.
```

## 1. Systems thinking, general systems theory, cybernetics

[Wiener1948]
  Wiener, N. (1948). *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in
  the Animal and the Machine*. MIT Press.

[Ashby1956]
  Ashby, W.R. (1956). *An Introduction to Cybernetics*. Chapman &
  Hall. — Source of the Law of Requisite Variety: a controller must
  have at least as much variety as the process it controls.

[Simon1962]
  Simon, H.A. (1962). "The Architecture of Complexity."
  *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society*, 106(6),
  467–482. — Nested, nearly decomposable hierarchies as the
  signature structure of complex systems.

[KatzKahn1966]
  Katz, D. & Kahn, R.L. (1966). *The Social Psychology of
  Organizations*. Wiley. — The classic open-systems treatment of
  organisations: inputs, throughputs, outputs, and environment.

[Bertalanffy1968]
  von Bertalanffy, L. (1968). *General System Theory: Foundations,
  Development, Applications*. George Braziller.

[Churchman1968]
  Churchman, C.W. (1968). *The Systems Approach*. Delacorte Press.
  — Early insistence that the system boundary is an analytical
  choice, not a fact about the world.

[Ackoff1971]
  Ackoff, R.L. (1971). "Towards a System of Systems Concepts."
  *Management Science*, 17(11), 661–671.

[Anderson1972]
  Anderson, P.W. (1972). "More Is Different." *Science*, 177(4047),
  393–396. — The canonical statement that new levels of organisation
  bring genuinely new behaviour.

[Beer1972]
  Beer, S. (1972). *Brain of the Firm*. Allen Lane. — The Viable
  System Model: a recursive, invariant control structure claimed to
  underlie every viable organisation.

[Forrester1971]
  Forrester, J.W. (1971). "Counterintuitive Behavior of Social
  Systems." *Technology Review*, 73(3), 52–68.

[Beer1979]
  Beer, S. (1979). *The Heart of Enterprise*. Wiley.

[MaturanaVarela1980]
  Maturana, H.R. & Varela, F.J. (1980). *Autopoiesis and Cognition:
  The Realization of the Living*. Reidel. — System identity as
  continuity of organisation rather than of components.

[Checkland1981]
  Checkland, P. (1981). *Systems Thinking, Systems Practice*. Wiley.
  — The hard/soft systems distinction; Soft Systems Methodology as
  the precedent for treating decompositions of human systems as
  models to be negotiated, not documents to be read off.

[Ulrich1983]
  Ulrich, W. (1983). *Critical Heuristics of Social Planning: A New
  Approach to Practical Philosophy*. Haupt. — Boundary critique:
  systematic questioning of whose concerns a chosen boundary
  includes and excludes.

[Luhmann1984]
  Luhmann, N. (1984). *Soziale Systeme: Grundriß einer allgemeinen
  Theorie*. Suhrkamp. (English: *Social Systems*, Stanford
  University Press, 1995.) — Social systems as self-referential
  communication systems; second-order observation (the observer of
  social systems is itself a social system).

[Simon1996]
  Simon, H.A. (1996). *The Sciences of the Artificial* (3rd ed.).
  MIT Press.

[Bedau1997]
  Bedau, M.A. (1997). "Weak Emergence." *Philosophical
  Perspectives*, 11, 375–399.

[Meadows1999]
  Meadows, D.H. (1999). *Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a
  System*. Sustainability Institute.
  https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/

[Beer2002]
  Beer, S. (2002). "What Is Cybernetics?" *Kybernetes*, 31(2),
  209–219. — Source of POSIWID: "the purpose of a system is what it
  does."

[Chalmers2006]
  Chalmers, D.J. (2006). "Strong and Weak Emergence." In Clayton, P.
  & Davies, P. (eds.), *The Re-Emergence of Emergence*. Oxford
  University Press.

[Meadows2008]
  Meadows, D.H. (2008). *Thinking in Systems: A Primer*. Chelsea
  Green. — Defines a system as elements, interconnections, and a
  function or purpose. Two distinct orderings run through this book
  and the companion leverage-points paper [Meadows1999], and merging
  them is a standing hazard: how much changing an element
  *transforms* the system, and how much *leverage* an intervention
  has on its behaviour. Neither is an ordering by how hard the change
  is to carry out. See `../foundations/system-definition.md`.

[Medina2011]
  Medina, E. (2011). *Cybernetic Revolutionaries: Technology and
  Politics in Allende's Chile*. MIT Press. — The historical record
  of Project Cybersyn: what happened when cybernetic management was
  applied to a national economy.

## 2. Safety science, STAMP/STPA, accident models

## 5. Institutions, governance, political economy

[Madison1788]
  Hamilton, A., Madison, J. & Jay, J. (1787–88). *The Federalist
  Papers*. — Especially No. 51 (Madison): "Ambition must be made to
  counteract ambition" — the classical statement of structural
  (rather than virtue-based) remedies for accountability voids.

[Michels1911]
  Michels, R. (1911). *Zur Soziologie des Parteiwesens in der
  modernen Demokratie*. Klinkhardt. (English: *Political Parties*.)
  — The "iron law of oligarchy": organisations claiming democratic
  goals drift toward rule by their own apparatus.

[Weber1922]
  Weber, M. (1922). *Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft*. Mohr. (English:
  *Economy and Society*, University of California Press, 1978.) —
  The tripartition of legitimate authority (charismatic,
  traditional, legal-rational) and the theory of bureaucracy.

[Merton1936]
  Merton, R.K. (1936). "The Unanticipated Consequences of Purposive
  Social Action." *American Sociological Review*, 1(6), 894–904.

[Selznick1949]
  Selznick, P. (1949). *TVA and the Grass Roots*. University of
  California Press. — Goal displacement: how an organisation's
  operative goals drift from its declared ones under environmental
  pressure.

[Lindblom1959]
  Lindblom, C.E. (1959). "The Science of 'Muddling Through'."
  *Public Administration Review*, 19(2), 79–88. — Incrementalism as
  the empirically observed (and defensible) mode of institutional
  change.

[Merton1968]
  Merton, R.K. (1968). *Social Theory and Social Structure*
  (enlarged ed.). Free Press. — Manifest vs latent functions: the
  sociological original of the declared-vs-operative-goals
  distinction.

[Hirschman1970]
  Hirschman, A.O. (1970). *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty: Responses to
  Decline in Firms, Organizations, and States*. Harvard University
  Press. — Feedback channels in social systems come in exactly two
  architectural families — leaving (exit) and speaking up (voice) —
  moderated by loyalty.

[Arendt1970]
  Arendt, H. (1970). *On Violence*. Harcourt Brace.

[RittelWebber1973]
  Rittel, H.W.J. & Webber, M.M. (1973). "Dilemmas in a General
  Theory of Planning." *Policy Sciences*, 4(2), 155–169. — Wicked
  problems: why social-system problems resist the
  define-solve-verify cycle of engineering.

[MeyerRowan1977]
  Meyer, J.W. & Rowan, B. (1977). "Institutionalized Organizations:
  Formal Structure as Myth and Ceremony." *American Journal of
  Sociology*, 83(2), 340–363. — Formal structures maintained for
  legitimacy, decoupled from actual operations; the
  institutional-sociology original of the claimed-vs-operating
  distinction.

[Ostrom1990]
  Ostrom, E. (1990). *Governing the Commons: The Evolution of
  Institutions for Collective Action*. Cambridge University Press. —
  Eight design principles of durable self-governed institutions,
  derived from field cases; the closest existing empirical programme
  to this project's cross-system platform analysis.

[North1990]
  North, D.C. (1990). *Institutions, Institutional Change and
  Economic Performance*. Cambridge University Press.

[Scott1998]
  Scott, J.C. (1998). *Seeing Like a State: How Certain Schemes to
  Improve the Human Condition Have Failed*. Yale University Press. —
  Legibility; the failure record of top-down, goal-first social
  redesign; *mētis* (local tacit knowledge) as what formal models
  omit.

[Ostrom2005]
  Ostrom, E. (2005). *Understanding Institutional Diversity*.
  Princeton University Press. — The Institutional Analysis and
  Development (IAD) framework and the grammar of institutions.

[Ostrom2007]
  Ostrom, E. (2007). "A Diagnostic Approach for Going beyond
  Panaceas." *PNAS*, 104(39), 15181–15187. — Against blueprint
  transfer of institutional designs; for diagnosis before remedy.

[AcemogluRobinson2012]
  Acemoglu, D. & Robinson, J.A. (2012). *Why Nations Fail: The
  Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty*. Crown. — Inclusive vs
  extractive institutions; why formal institutional copies fail
  without the underlying power distribution.

[Andrews2017]
  Andrews, M., Pritchett, L. & Woolcock, M. (2017). *Building State
  Capability: Evidence, Analysis, Action*. Oxford University Press.
  Open access: https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198747482.001.0001
  — "Isomorphic mimicry": institutions that adopt the *form* of
  high-performing systems to gain legitimacy while lacking their
  *function*. An independently developed empirical treatment of what
  this project calls the claimed-rung vs operating-rung gap.

## 7. Measurement, metrics, audit

[Goodhart1975]
  Goodhart, C.A.E. (1975). "Problems of Monetary Management: The
  U.K. Experience." In *Papers in Monetary Economics*, Vol. I.
  Reserve Bank of Australia. — Goodhart's law: any observed
  statistical regularity collapses once pressure is placed upon it
  for control purposes.

[Campbell1979]
  Campbell, D.T. (1979). "Assessing the Impact of Planned Social
  Change." *Evaluation and Program Planning*, 2(1), 67–90. —
  Campbell's law: the more a quantitative indicator is used for
  decision-making, the more it corrupts the process it monitors.

[Power1997]
  Power, M. (1997). *The Audit Society: Rituals of Verification*.
  Oxford University Press. — When audit becomes ritual: the
  conditions under which formal verification stops carrying
  information.

[Strathern1997]
  Strathern, M. (1997). "'Improving Ratings': Audit in the British
  University System." *European Review*, 5(3), 305–321. — "When a
  measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."

[Muller2018]
  Muller, J.Z. (2018). *The Tyranny of Metrics*. Princeton
  University Press.
