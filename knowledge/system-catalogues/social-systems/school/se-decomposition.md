# School — SE Decomposition

The canonical five-level SE decomposition of the school (secondary
education) as a social system. The school is the worked example used
in the book's introduction (`projects/systems-introduction-book/docs/introduction.md`)
to motivate the difference between declared goals and operative goals.

## Goals (G)

- **G1** — Develop students' capacity to think, reason, create, and contribute
- **G2** — Transmit shared knowledge, culture, and civic capacity to the next generation
- **G3** — Select and signal student capability to subsequent stages (university, vocational, employment)
- **G4** — Provide safe, structured care for school-age children while parents work
- **G5** — Equalise opportunity across socioeconomic background (where mandated as a goal)

## Requirements (R)

- **R1** — Qualified teaching staff with pedagogical training
- **R2** — Defined curriculum aligned to age-appropriate developmental milestones
- **R3** — Assessment instruments capable of measuring R2 attainment
- **R4** — Pastoral care structures for safeguarding and welfare
- **R5** — Statutory and inspectorate framework defining minimum standards
- **R6** — Financial sustainability through state funding and/or fees
- **R7** — Facilities (classrooms, library, sports, science) appropriate to curriculum
- **R8** — Interface with parents/guardians as co-responsible adults

## Functions (F)

- **F1** — Curriculum delivery — teach the syllabus through lessons, projects, practice
- **F2** — Assessment — measure individual pupil attainment for feedback and selection
- **F3** — Pastoral care — safeguard pupils' welfare, mental and physical health
- **F4** — Behavioural management — establish norms, address disciplinary cases
- **F5** — Resource management — staffing, budget, facilities, timetable
- **F6** — External liaison — parents, inspectorate, ministry, partner institutions
- **F7** — Selection signalling — produce qualifications/transcripts used for next-stage decisions
- **F8** — Co-curricular activity — sport, arts, civic engagement, school trips

## Logical Architecture (L)

- **L1** — Pedagogical subsystem — curriculum, teaching teams, lesson plans, materials
- **L2** — Assessment subsystem — internal tests, national exams, transcripts, mark schemes
- **L3** — Pastoral subsystem — form tutors, year heads, counsellors, safeguarding lead
- **L4** — Management subsystem — headteacher, deputies, department heads, governance board
- **L5** — Administration subsystem — registrar, timetable, finance, HR, facilities
- **L6** — External liaison subsystem — parent communications, inspectorate interface, university advisors

## Physical Implementation (P)

- **P1** — Teachers — subject specialists, form tutors, support staff, teaching assistants
- **P2** — Headteacher / principal and senior leadership team
- **P3** — Governance board / school board / Schulleitung-equivalent body
- **P4** — Buildings — classrooms, labs, library, sports facilities, kitchen, offices
- **P5** — Curriculum documents, lesson plans, textbooks, digital resources
- **P6** — Assessment instruments — internal tests, national exams (GCSE, Abitur, baccalauréat, …)
- **P7** — Information systems — pupil registry (SIMS, ASV, equivalents), timetable, MIS
- **P8** — Inspectorate — Ofsted, Schulaufsicht, equivalent — as external observer
- **P9** — Pupils themselves — registered, year-grouped, subject to compulsory attendance
- **P10** — Parents/guardians as a contractual or statutory counter-party

## Key Structural Observations

**G1 (capacity) and G3 (selection) are different goals served by the same instrument.**
Assessment (F2) feeds both the formative loop that adjusts teaching (G1)
and the summative signal that selects pupils for the next stage (G3).
When stakes attach asymmetrically to the selection use, the formative
use is structurally crowded out. This is the canonical *proxy-metric*
pathology in the book's cross-system catalogue.

**R1 (qualified teachers) is the most consequential requirement.**
The variation point at which the school system differentiates most
sharply across jurisdictions is the level of teacher qualification,
pay, status, and autonomy — and the Finnish reform of the 1990s
isolates exactly this as the lever.

**The system has very weak rung-3 feedback on G1.**
There is no measurement of whether a 14-year-old becomes a flourishing
30-year-old that can be made on a timescale the school can act on.
The system therefore relies on proxies whose validity for G1 is
contested, and whose validity for G3 is unambiguous — which biases
the structure's measurement choices toward G3.
